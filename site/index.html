    HOW IT WORKS

    EVERY NAMED ROCK IS REAL

    The asteroids with names come from NASA/JPL's Center for Near-Earth Object Studies (CNEOS), which tracks every known asteroid flyby. The data refreshes every night.

    The game is the data

    • Size sets how big a rock looks and how many hits it takes.
    • Speed sets how fast it falls.
    • Miss distance sets the order in Today's Sky. The closest flyby comes last.
    • Potentially hazardous asteroids become boss fights.

    Purple debris is made up, to keep things busy. Amber and red rocks are real.

    Learn by playing

    • See it: when you destroy a real asteroid, you see its size drawn to scale next to something familiar, like a bus, a football field or the Eiffel Tower.
    • Radar: the dial in the corner shows where each asteroid really passed, compared with the Moon's orbit.
    • Feel it: if one slips past, the impact flash shows what a real hit would equal.
    • Optional extras: between waves, open the field notes for the full story, or tap Guess to predict something about the asteroid for bonus points. You can also ignore both and keep playing.
    • Badges are named after real ideas, like Inside the Moon or Tunguska Stopper. Your logbook keeps every real asteroid you destroy.

    Modes

    • Today's Sky: today's real flybys. Everyone gets the same level, so you can compare scores.
    • On This Day: close flybys on today's calendar date from 1900 to 2100, in time order. Future ones are forecasts.
    • Legends: famous flybys, from Toutatis to Apophis in 2029.

    Units

    LD means lunar distance: 384,400 km, the average distance to the Moon. Miss distances are measured from Earth's center, as JPL reports them. Sizes marked ≈ are estimated from brightness, so the true size can differ.

    Data: NASA/JPL CNEOS close-approach data. Impact energies are rough estimates for a stony asteroid. The leaderboard stores only the name you type and your scores; everything else stays on this device. Made by Rohan Tucker.
